# CPTS vs OSCP: Which Exam Is Actually Harder?

There is one question that keeps coming up whenever people compare penetration testing certifications:

> **"CPTS or OSCP — which one is harder?"**

And honestly, it is not as simple as picking one.

You will find people saying CPTS is harder.

Others will tell you OSCP is harder.

Then someone will say:

> "Just do both."

Helpful.

Except now you have two certifications to prepare for and even more confusion. 😂

So instead of giving you a lazy **"CPTS is harder"** or **"OSCP is harder"** answer, let's look at where each certification actually becomes difficult.

## First, They Test You Differently

Both are practical penetration testing certifications, but the experience is not identical.

The current **OSCP+** exam gives candidates **23 hours and 45 minutes** for the practical exam, followed by another **24 hours for documentation**. The exam includes three standalone machines and an Active Directory environment, with **70/100 required to pass**. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

CPTS takes a different approach.

Hack The Box currently gives candidates **10 days** for the CPTS exam, and a professional penetration testing report is part of the submission. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

That difference alone changes how the pressure feels.

OSCP says:

> **"You have almost a day. Make your decisions carefully."**

CPTS gives you considerably more time, but that doesn't mean you can relax.

You still have to solve the problems.

---

# Learning Material: CPTS Has an Advantage

This is one area where CPTS stands out.

The HTB Academy ecosystem is heavily focused on structured, hands-on learning.

You learn a concept.

You practice it.

You encounter it again.

Then you use it somewhere else.

That repetition can make the learning experience feel very deep.

For someone who learns by doing, this can be extremely effective.

OSCP is also highly practical, but the experience feels more focused on developing a penetration-testing methodology and preparing you to operate independently.

So if your question is:

> **"Which one gives me a huge amount of structured practical learning?"**

CPTS has a very strong argument.

---

# Lab Difficulty: Don't Underestimate CPTS

This is where the CPTS reputation comes from.

HTB is not exactly famous for giving people easy machines and a participation trophy.

The learning path can take you through a large amount of practical material.

You are expected to understand what you're doing, not simply copy commands.

And that can be uncomfortable.

You might spend a long time on a problem.

You might understand the vulnerability but still struggle to turn it into access.

You might have to research something completely new.

And eventually you get that moment:

> **"Wait... THAT was the problem?"**

Those moments are frustrating.

But they are also where a lot of the learning happens.

---

# Enumeration: Both Can Hurt

If there is one skill you should not underestimate in either certification, it is enumeration.

You can know dozens of exploitation techniques.

It won't matter much if you don't properly understand your target.

Good enumeration means:

- Finding services
- Understanding versions
- Identifying attack surfaces
- Discovering users and shares
- Investigating web applications
- Connecting seemingly unrelated information

The difficult part is not running a scanner.

The difficult part is looking at the results and asking:

> **"What does this actually tell me?"**

That mindset matters in both exams.

---

# Exam Methodology: OSCP Feels More Compressed

This is where OSCP can become mentally difficult.

You have a concentrated exam window.

You start working.

You find something.

You investigate it.

It doesn't work.

You try another approach.

And suddenly several hours have disappeared.

The current OSCP+ structure has three standalone targets plus a three-machine AD set, and you need to manage your time across those objectives while also keeping documentation in mind. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

That creates a very specific type of pressure.

You cannot afford to spend the entire exam chasing one idea.

At some point, you have to make a decision:

> **"Is this worth another hour?"**

That is a skill by itself.

---

# CPTS: More Time Doesn't Mean Easy

The 10-day CPTS window sounds much more comfortable.

And in one way, it is.

You don't have the same concentrated clock pressure as OSCP.

But don't make the mistake of thinking:

> "Ten days means CPTS is easier."

It doesn't.

You still need to understand the environment.

You still need to enumerate.

You still need to exploit.

You still need to troubleshoot.

And you still need to produce professional documentation. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

The pressure is simply different.

OSCP can feel like:

> **"I need to make good decisions quickly."**

CPTS can feel more like:

> **"I have time, but I still need to solve this properly."**

---

# Reporting: The Part People Forget

This is one of those things that looks easy until you actually have to do it.

You finish an attack.

Great.

Now explain it.

What did you find?

How did you get initial access?

How did you escalate?

What evidence proves it?

What was the impact?

How would you remediate it?

Both certifications expect professional documentation.

For OSCP+, the exam report must clearly document the exploitation process, including relevant steps, commands, output, screenshots, and results. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}

HTB likewise requires a professional penetration testing report for CPTS. :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}

So if you are preparing for either exam, don't treat reporting as an afterthought.

Make documentation part of your practice.

---

# The Real Enemy: Rabbit Holes

This is probably where both exams feel surprisingly similar.

You find something interesting.

You convince yourself it must be important.

You spend another hour.

Then another.

And eventually:

> **"Why am I still doing this?"**

That is the rabbit hole.

The ability to recognize one is extremely important.

Sometimes the best move is not another exploit.

It is stepping back and enumerating again.

Look at the bigger picture.

Try another target.

Come back later.

A practical certification is not just testing whether you know attacks.

It is testing whether you can **think through a problem**.

# So Which One Is Actually Harder?

Here is the honest answer:

## **It depends on what you find difficult.**

### CPTS may feel harder if:

- You prefer guided learning and struggle with independent research.
- Deep technical material takes you longer to absorb.
- You are not comfortable with HTB-style challenges.
- You tend to rely heavily on walkthroughs.
- You struggle when the solution is not obvious.

### OSCP+ may feel harder if:

- You struggle with time pressure.
- You spend too long on rabbit holes.
- You are uncomfortable making decisions independently.
- You struggle with Active Directory.
- You find concentrated practical exams stressful.

So when someone says:

> **"CPTS is harder."**

or:

> **"OSCP is harder."**

Take it with a grain of salt.

They may simply be describing **what was harder for them personally**.

---

# Which One Should You Take First?

If you are choosing between the two, don't start with difficulty.

Start with your goal.

### Choose CPTS if:

You want a deep, structured, hands-on learning experience and enjoy the HTB ecosystem.

### Choose OSCP+ if:

You want a well-known practical certification and want to test your skills under a much more concentrated exam environment.

And if you're still building your fundamentals?

You might not need either one yet.

That is not a weakness.

It is actually better to realize that **before** paying for an exam.

# Final Verdict

If you want one simple answer:

**CPTS can feel harder from a depth and learning perspective.**

**OSCP+ can feel harder from an exam-pressure and decision-making perspective.**

Neither is simply "the harder certification."

They challenge you in different ways.

And honestly, that is probably a better answer than declaring one winner.

Because the certification that feels hardest isn't necessarily the one with the hardest syllabus.

It is the one that exposes **your weakest skills**.

So before asking:

> **"Which exam is harder?"**

Ask yourself:

> **"Which type of challenge am I ready for?"**

That answer will probably tell you which one you should take next.
