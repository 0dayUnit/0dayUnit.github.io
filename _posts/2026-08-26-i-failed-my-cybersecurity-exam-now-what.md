Let's talk about something you don't see enough in the cybersecurity community.

**Failure.**

Most of the time, you see the other side.

The certificate.

The celebration.

The screenshot saying:

> "Finally passed!"

And that's great.

But what happens when the screen says:

> **Failed.**

No certificate.

No celebration.

**"What do I do now?"**

First, take a breath.

Failing one exam doesn't mean you're bad at cybersecurity.

It means **you didn't pass this attempt.**

That's it.

---

## Don't Book the Retake Immediately

It's tempting.

You fail and your first thought is:

> "I'll book another attempt and pass it this time."

Wait.

Before spending more money, figure out **why you failed**.

Was it:

- Lack of technical knowledge?
- Poor enumeration?
- Time management?
- Getting stuck too long?
- Weak reporting?
- Exam pressure?

If you don't know what went wrong, another attempt may simply produce the same result.

---

## Turn Your Result Into a Plan

If you receive a score or performance breakdown, don't treat it like a judgment.

Treat it like evidence.

Ask yourself:

> **"Where was my biggest weakness?"**

If your technical skills were strong but you struggled with reporting, improve your documentation.

If Active Directory caused problems, spend more time there.

If you ran out of time, work on your methodology and time management.

Your result should tell you **what to work on next**.

---

## Should You Retake Immediately?

It depends.

If you were close to passing and already know exactly what went wrong, a short period of focused preparation might be enough.

But if you finished the exam thinking:

> "I had no idea what I was doing."

Then don't rush back into another attempt.

Practice first.

Fix the gaps.

Then try again.

**Retake when you're prepared, not when you're frustrated.**

---

## Don't Let Someone Else's Success Get Into Your Head

This part hurts more than people admit.

You fail your exam.

Then you open LinkedIn.

Someone else just passed OSCP.

Someone got CPTS.

Someone completed another certification in three months.

And suddenly you're wondering:

> "Maybe I'm not good enough."

Don't do that to yourself.

You're comparing your **worst moment** with someone else's **best moment**.

You don't know their previous attempts, experience, preparation, or struggles.

Your timeline is your own.

---

## What Should You Do Now?

Keep it simple.

**1. Analyse the failure.**  
Find the exact areas that caused problems.

**2. Fix the gaps.**  
Don't spend all your time practicing what you already know.

**3. Practice without walkthroughs.**  
Learn to solve problems independently.

**4. Practice under time pressure.**  
Technical skills mean little if you cannot manage the clock.

**5. Try again when you're ready.**

Not because someone else passed.

Not because you're angry.

**Because you're ready.**

# Final Thought

Failing an exam hurts.

That's normal.

But one failed attempt doesn't erase everything you learned while preparing.

You now know where the gaps are.

You know what exam pressure feels like.

And you know what you need to improve.

So don't ask:

> **"Why did I fail?"**

> **"What am I going to do differently next time?"**

Then get back to work.

**One failed attempt is not the end of your cybersecurity journey.**
