# OSCP vs CPTS: Which One Should You Choose First?

If you're getting serious about penetration testing, you've probably reached this point:

And honestly, it's a fair question.

Both names keep appearing everywhere.

Someone tells you OSCP is the gold standard.

Someone else says CPTS is harder.

Then someone says CPTS teaches you more.

And five minutes later, you've opened twelve Reddit tabs and somehow you're more confused than when you started.

So let's make this simple.

**OSCP or CPTS?**

There isn't one answer for everyone.

But depending on where you are right now, one of them may make much more sense than the other.

---

## First, What Are We Comparing?

### OSCP+

OSCP+ is OffSec's practical penetration-testing certification.

The current exam gives you **23 hours and 45 minutes** to work through the exam environment, followed by another **24 hours to submit your documentation**. The exam is worth 100 points and requires **70 points to pass**. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

The current structure includes:

- 3 standalone machines
- An Active Directory set containing 3 machines
- Initial access
- Privilege escalation
- Active Directory exploitation
- Professional reporting

So yes, it's practical.

And yes, the clock is real.

---

### CPTS

HTB's **Certified Penetration Testing Specialist (CPTS)** is also heavily practical.

HTB currently describes CPTS as an **intermediate-level, hands-on penetration testing certification** that evaluates technical competency and requires candidates to produce a professional penetration-testing report. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

The interesting difference is the learning ecosystem around it.

HTB's Penetration Tester Job Role Path is specifically designed to prepare learners for CPTS. HTB currently estimates the complete path at around **342 hours** of learning content. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

That's a lot of material.

And that's actually one of the biggest reasons people compare CPTS with OSCP.

---

# OSCP vs CPTS: Difficulty

Let's get the controversial part out of the way.

> **Which one is harder?**

You'll find people arguing about this forever.

And honestly, difficulty isn't a single number.

CPTS can feel extremely challenging because of the **depth and amount of material** you work through.

OSCP can feel extremely challenging because of the **exam pressure, methodology, time management, and practical decision-making required during the assessment**.

So instead of asking:

> "Which one is harder?"

Ask:

> **"Which kind of difficulty am I better prepared for?"**

If you like structured learning and want to spend a huge amount of time building your skills through guided material and labs, CPTS can be very attractive.

If you want a certification challenge with strong industry recognition and you're comfortable working under a concentrated exam environment, OSCP+ becomes very interesting.

---

# Learning Experience

This is probably the biggest difference.

## CPTS: Learn → Practice → Learn More

HTB's Academy ecosystem is heavily lab-driven.

You move through modules, exercises, challenges, and a dedicated job-role path.

You're constantly doing something.

Read.

Test.

Break something.

Fix it.

Try again.

That style works extremely well for people who learn by doing.

And if you enjoy HTB already, you may naturally feel at home here.

---

## OSCP: Learn → Practice → Solve

OffSec's PEN-200 path is also practical, but the certification is strongly associated with developing a methodology for approaching penetration-testing problems.

The exam itself is not there to hold your hand.

At some point, you're alone with the targets.

That's when things get interesting.

You enumerate.

You find something.

You try it.

It doesn't work.

You try something else.

Suddenly three hours have disappeared.

Welcome to pentesting.

---

# Exam Style

Here's where the difference becomes very obvious.

### OSCP+

You have a concentrated exam window of **23 hours and 45 minutes**, followed by the reporting period. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}

The exam includes standalone targets and an Active Directory environment.

You need to manage:

- Time
- Enumeration
- Exploitation
- Privilege escalation
- AD methodology
- Documentation

And you need to make decisions without someone telling you what to try next.

---

### CPTS

CPTS works differently.

HTB currently gives candidates **10 days** for the CPTS exam window. The exam also requires professional documentation, and the report must be submitted as part of the certification process. :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}

That doesn't mean CPTS is "easy."

Far from it.

It simply means the pressure is distributed differently.

Instead of having one extremely concentrated 24-hour-style challenge, you're working within a much longer exam window.

For some people, that's a huge advantage.

For others, having ten days might simply mean ten days of staring at the same machine.

😂

# What About Cost?

This is where you should **always check the current official pricing before buying**, because certification pricing and packages can change.

HTB currently lists the CPTS exam voucher at **$210 before VAT**, while its current Silver Annual subscription is listed at **$490/year** and includes access to the Penetration Tester path plus an exam voucher under the current offering. :contentReference[oaicite:6]{index=6}

OffSec pricing depends on the package you choose, so don't compare only the exam price.

Look at the **total investment**:

That's the number that actually matters.

And your time is probably the most expensive part.

Which One Is Better for a Beginner?
===================================

This is where I wouldn't blindly recommend either one.

If you're completely new to penetration testing, don't choose a certification simply because someone told you it's the best.

First ask yourself:

*   Do I understand networking?
    
*   Am I comfortable with Linux?
    
*   Can I enumerate a target properly?
    
*   Do I understand basic web attacks?
    
*   Can I perform basic privilege escalation?
    
*   Have I actually spent time in labs?
    

If the answer to most of these is **no**, build your fundamentals first.

Because jumping into a difficult certification too early doesn't make you more advanced.

It just makes the learning curve steeper.

Which One Is Better for Real-World Skills?
==========================================

Here's the thing.

**Both can teach you valuable practical skills.**

CPTS has a very extensive structured path and is designed around hands-on penetration-testing competency.

OSCP+ tests practical exploitation, privilege escalation, Active Directory, and professional reporting under a concentrated exam environment.

So don't think:

> CPTS = skillsOSCP = certificate

That's an unfair comparison.

Both require actual technical work.

The better question is which learning environment fits **your learning style and career goal**.

And What About Jobs?
====================

This is where people sometimes overthink certifications.

A certification can help your resume.

It can help you get noticed.

It can give you something concrete to demonstrate.

But neither OSCP nor CPTS is a magic **"get pentesting job"** button.

A hiring manager may still care about:

*   Practical skills
    
*   Methodology
    
*   Reporting
    
*   Communication
    
*   Labs and projects
    
*   Previous experience
    
*   Problem-solving
    

So don't spend thousands expecting a certificate to do all the work for you.

The certificate should support your skills.

Not replace them.

So... OSCP or CPTS?
===================

Here's my simple take.

### Choose CPTS first if:

*   You prefer structured, hands-on learning.
    
*   You enjoy HTB Academy.
    
*   You want a very extensive practical learning path.
    
*   You want more time during the exam window.
    
*   You're focused heavily on building penetration-testing skills.
    

### Choose OSCP+ first if:

*   You want strong industry recognition.
    
*   You're comfortable with practical pentesting.
    
*   You want a concentrated certification challenge.
    
*   You want to test your methodology under time pressure.
    
*   OSCP aligns directly with the roles you're targeting.
    

And there's another perfectly valid answer:

> **Do neither yet.**

If you're not ready, spend some time in labs first.

There's no shame in that.

Actually, it might save you money.

My Verdict
==========

If you asked me:

> **"Which one should I take first?"**

I'd say:

### **If your priority is structured learning → CPTS.**

### **If your priority is certification recognition + a focused practical challenge → OSCP+.**

But don't choose based on someone else's LinkedIn post.

Choose based on **where you are right now**.

Your friend passing OSCP doesn't mean you're ready for OSCP.

Someone finishing CPTS doesn't mean CPTS is automatically the right choice for you.

And if you're sitting there thinking:

> "Okay... but which one actually makes sense for _me_?"

That's probably the more important question.
